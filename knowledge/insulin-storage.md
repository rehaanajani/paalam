# Insulin storage during power cuts and floods

Source: product leaflet of the insulin in use
General guidance found on most insulin product leaflets. Storage limits differ by product: always check the leaflet or ask a pharmacist.

## Typical storage rules
- Unopened insulin is usually kept in a refrigerator, not frozen.
- Insulin in use is usually fine at room temperature (often up to about 30°C) for a limited number of days stated on the leaflet, commonly around four weeks.
- Never freeze insulin. Insulin that has frozen should not be used.
- Keep it away from direct sunlight, heat sources and flood water.

## During a power cut
- A closed fridge stays cool for some hours; open it as little as possible.
- If the room is hot, keep insulin in a cool, shaded place or an insulated bag with a cool (not frozen) pack. Don't let ice touch it.
- Don't use insulin that looks cloudy when it should be clear, has clumps or crystals, or has changed colour.

## When to get help
- If doses are missed, or the person shows signs of very high or very low blood sugar (confusion, drowsiness, sweating, vomiting, unusual breathing), call 108.
- Don't change doses without a doctor's advice.
